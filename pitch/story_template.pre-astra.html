The puzzle

Same building. Same bedrooms. Very different rents.

Suburb medians can't explain that, and neither can a floor number. To find out what can, I needed to know what every flat looks out on.

$1,000a week apart

Largest raw gap between asking rents in one building, same bedroom count (3-bed, 8 flats), Jun–Jul 2026 adverts. Unadjusted; small groups; buildings not named.

Rebuilding the city

I rebuilt the city from laser scans and traced the view from every floor.

Two public NSW LiDAR surveys, 2013 and 2020, at one metre. From each floor of every residential building I cast 36 sightlines, one every 10°, out to 1.5 km in 5 m steps. Towers and trees block them. Water and parks are recorded where a line reaches them.

What the view is worth

The building sets most of the price. The view separates flats inside it.

15.5%

of measured floors can see water in at least one direction.

≈ +4%

more rent, within the same building, for water in a quarter of the directions.

2,912

buildings compared from the inside, across 22,905 adverts.

Within-building estimate on the Jun–Jul 2026 holdout: 0.168 log points per full share of water directions; no confidence interval yet. Reading it as "building first, view second" is an interpretation of this small coefficient beside the strong same-building comparables, not a measured decomposition.

How a rent is built

Each flat is priced from the best evidence it has.

That finding shaped the model. Price the flat from its own history when it is fresh, then from its building, then its suburb, and use the view only to adjust within the building.

Its own past adverts

The latest same-bedroom advert, moved to today by a same-flat repeat-rent index.

Only while it is fresh

Own history older than 36 months is set aside. 75% of flats with history last advertised over a year ago.

Its building

Same bedrooms, latest advert per neighbouring flat: one flat, one vote.

Its view, inside the building

A small LiDAR adjustment for the difference between this flat's floor and its neighbours'.

Its suburb

Suburb, bedrooms and parking, when nothing closer exists. Rounded to $5, as 84% of real asking rents end in 0.

$45 is v1, the hackathon submission. The ranges are v2, a revision made after that check, selected on pre-cut-off walk-forward data only, locked by hash, scored once; 81.5% covered over 1 Oct–1 Nov. Misses fall more often below the range (14.2%) than above (5.5%), consistent with rents easing after July. Own sample of later adverts via the Microburbs API; asking rents. On the Jun–Jul holdout: $45 against $46.1 for gradient-boosted trees on the same features, $97 for suburb medians.

What I'd build next

Three next steps.

Ranges by evidence and horizon

Check coverage, width and bias separately for own-history, building and suburb estimates, on a shared evaluation set.

A trend adjustment after the cut-off

Misses lean below the range. A market-trend adjustment for the months after the data stops is the clear next fix.

Views with confidence

Put a confidence interval on the view effect, then test whether same-flat growth and suburb medians diverge where new towers rose.
